FPS Multiplayer Game

A multiplayer first-person shooter built with Unity and C# that supports 2+ players over LAN or the internet using PUN2 Photon networking. My deep dive into networked gameplay.

In progress — playable with friends
Source code
01 · idea

The idea

Most single-player FPS tutorials stop at shooting bots. I wanted something with real people in the session — simultaneous movement, shooting, and reactions that only come from a live opponent.

The goal: a small arena FPS where a group of friends can join, see each other move and shoot in real time, and have the match actually feel responsive.

02 · role

My role

I built the project end to end, but the part I care most about is the networking:

  • Photon PUN2 networking — rooms, connect flow, and joining players over LAN/internet.
  • Player movement and shooting as networked actions, not just local visuals.
  • Match flow — spawn, combat, and score/respawn handling across clients.
03 · tech

Technologies used

Unity C# PUN2 / Photon

Unity with C# for all gameplay logic, and Photon PUN2 for the networking layer. Room-based architecture means a small lobby that friends can join directly — no dedicated matchmaking server needed.

04 · challenges

Challenges

Networking breaks the single-player mindset. State has to be shared, ownership has to be explicit (who moves this object?), and smoothness depends on interpolation and latency handling rather than raw speed. Debugging "works solo but breaks with two clients" moments taught me more about real-time systems than any static scene.

05 · outcome

Outcome & lessons

A playable multiplayer FPS where multiple humans share a session — movement, combat, and connection flow all working. Still in progress as I add maps and modes. The main takeaway: think in authority and state, not just in frames.
