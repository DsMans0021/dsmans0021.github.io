Maya

A complete Unity game shipped on itch.io — my first fully polished, publicly playable release. This page walks through the idea, my role, the stack, and what I learned.

Play live demo Source code
01 · idea

The idea

Maya started as a game jam instinct: ship something small that feels finished. The result is a complete Unity game with custom gameplay mechanics, a real level flow, and the kind of polish people only notice when it's missing — menus, audio, feedback, and a satisfying game-over / win loop.

It was published on itch.io so anyone could play it in the browser, which forced me to care about performance and early input handling too.

02 · role

My role

I designed, programmed, and shipped the game. My focus areas:

  • Gameplay systems in C# — movement, interactions, and win/lose conditions.
  • Level design and pacing so the game stays fun beyond the first minute.
  • UI, audio integration, and overall presentation — the difference between a demo and a release.
03 · tech

Technologies used

Unity C# itch.io

Built entirely in Unity with C# — scene management, player scripting, physics, and UI. Published through itch.io's browser embedding for instant play.

04 · challenges

Challenges

The hardest part was scope control: it's easy to keep adding systems and never finish. I had to cut features to protect the deadline, then spend the remaining time on polish — which is exactly the trade-off real studio work forces you to make.

05 · outcome

Outcome & lessons

A public, playable release that people can actually pick up and try. The biggest lesson: a finished small game teaches you more than an abandoned ambitious one. It also became the foundation of the itch.io presence I use for every jam since.
